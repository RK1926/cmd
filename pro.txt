Step 1.
 What programs are running? (linux_pslist)
diff <(awk 'NR>2{print $2}' c_pslist.txt | sort -u) <(awk 'NR>2{print $2}' i_pslist.txt | sort -u)

Step 2.
 Who started whom? (linux_pstree)
diff -u c_pstree.txt i_pstree.txt

Step 3.
(i) What exactly did they run? (linux_psaux)
diff <(awk 'NR>1{print}' c_psaux.txt | sort) \
     <(awk 'NR>1{print}' i_psaux.txt | sort)

Step 4. 
Did anyone become root?
grep -E "sudo|bash|wget|sleep" i_pslist.txt

Step 5.
 Prove where root came from (linux_psenv)
volatility -f "$I" --profile="$P" linux_psenv -p [PID] ==> ex 596

Step 6.
 What files does it have open? (linux_lsof)
(i)volatility -f $I --profile=$P linux_lsof -p [PID]
or
(ii)grep -w '596' i_lsof.txt ==> easier but must be in directory where txt files are


Step 7.
What did the users type? (linux_bash)
cat i_bash.txt

Step 8. Recover a script
get the address from psaux by matching pid from other ps_

then run :
volatility -f $I --profile=$P linux_find_file -i 0xffff977619987828 -O out/mirror_sync.sh

or

Enumerate files
volatility -f "$I" --profile="$P" linux_enumerate_files > ~/out/i_files.txt

Search for your script
grep -F "mirror_sync.sh" ~/out/i_files.txt

Extract the script
volatility -f "$I" --profile="$P" linux_find_file -i 0xffff977619987828 -O ~/out/mirror_sync.sh

Display the script's contents
cat ~/out/mirror_sync.sh







Hunting for suspicious things (Processes)

-Download or network tools	
grep -E "wget|curl|nc |ncat|netcat|ssh" i_psaux.txt

-Scripting and encoding
grep -E "python|perl|base64|chmod" i_psaux.txt

-Programs in temp folders
grep -E "/tmp|/dev/shm|/var/tmp" i_psaux.txt

-Normal users’ processes	
awk 'NR>2 && $5>=1000' i_pslist.txt

-Library injection
grep -E "LD_PRELOAD|LD_LIBRARY_PATH" i_psenv.txt

-Open files in temp or deleted
grep -E "/tmp|/dev/shm|deleted" i_lsof.txt

-Risky typed commands
grep -E "sudo|su |useradd|adduser|passwd|crontab|wget|curl|insmod" i_bash.txt

-Hidden processes
volatility -f $I --profile=$P linux_psxview
